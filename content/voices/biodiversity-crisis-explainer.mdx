## The numbers

The IPBES Global Assessment found that 1 million animal and plant species are currently threatened with extinction. The average abundance of native species has fallen by at least 20% since 1900. The current extinction rate is 100–1,000 times higher than the natural background rate.

## What is causing it?

Five direct drivers, ranked by impact:

1. **Land and sea use change** — converting natural ecosystems into farmland and cities
2. **Direct exploitation** — unsustainable hunting, fishing, and harvesting
3. **Climate change** — growing rapidly as a driver
4. **Pollution** — nitrogen runoff, plastics, chemical contamination
5. **Invasive species** — non-native species introduced by human activity

## Why biodiversity matters beyond ethics

Ecosystem services worth tens of trillions of dollars annually depend on biodiversity — pollination of 35% of global food production, water purification by wetlands and healthy soils, coastal protection by mangroves, medicines derived from natural compounds, and food security for over a billion people who depend on wild-caught fish.

## Tipping points

The Amazon faces a dieback threshold — a point where deforestation combined with climate-driven drought tips portions from rainforest to savanna. Scientists estimate this threshold at 20–25% deforestation. The Amazon is already past 17%.

## Reason for cautious hope

Rewilding projects across Europe have demonstrated rapid ecosystem recovery. Whale populations have rebounded since whaling moratoriums. The California condor, reduced to 27 individuals in 1987, now numbers over 500.
