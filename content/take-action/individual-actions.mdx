## The evidence on individual impact

The most effective individuals are those who reduce their own footprint *and* engage politically. This guide covers what research shows are the highest-impact individual actions.

## The highest-impact actions

Research from Lund University has identified actions with the largest per-person impact:

**Live car-free or switch to electric.** Cars account for roughly 20% of individual carbon footprints in high-income countries. Switching to public transport, cycling, or an electric vehicle powered by renewable energy cuts this dramatically.

**Avoid flying.** A single transatlantic return flight generates approximately one tonne of CO₂-equivalent. For frequent flyers, this is likely the single largest item in a personal carbon budget.

**Shift your diet.** Eliminating or dramatically reducing beef and lamb consumption is the highest-impact dietary change. A fully plant-based diet reduces food-related emissions by roughly 50%.

**Switch to renewable energy.** Choosing a certified renewable energy tariff eliminates the emissions from your home energy use.

## Medium-impact changes

- Buy less, choose well — consumer goods account for 10–15% of personal carbon footprints
- Bank and invest ethically — switch to banks that do not invest in fossil fuels
- Reduce food waste — the average household wastes roughly 30% of the food it buys

## Systemic engagement

Vote on environmental grounds. Support effective organisations working on systemic change. Use your professional skills in the transition — in your workplace, your profession, or volunteering.
