## The scale of food production

Global agriculture currently occupies 50% of Earth's habitable land, accounts for 26% of global greenhouse gas emissions, uses 70% of global freshwater withdrawals, and is the primary driver of 80% of global deforestation. Despite this footprint, roughly 820 million people remain food-insecure.

## The land use paradox

77% of all agricultural land is used for livestock — including pasture and feed crops. Yet livestock provides only 18% of the world's calories. A gram of protein from beef requires roughly 20 times more land than a gram from legumes.

## Deforestation: the hidden cost of food

In the Amazon, an estimated 80% of deforestation is driven by cattle ranching. Palm oil plantations have replaced vast swathes of rainforest in Borneo and Sumatra. The Amazon alone holds roughly 150–200 billion tonnes of carbon.

## The fertiliser problem

Synthetic nitrogen fertilisers made modern food production possible — but nitrogen that is not taken up by crops leaches into waterways and creates over 400 known coastal dead zones. Excess nitrogen also produces nitrous oxide, a greenhouse gas nearly 300 times more potent than CO₂.

## What a sustainable food system looks like

1. Shifting diets towards less beef and more legumes and vegetables
2. Reducing food waste — one-third of all food produced is lost or wasted
3. Improving yields on existing land to reduce pressure to clear new land
4. Scaling regenerative practices that build soil health and sequester carbon
