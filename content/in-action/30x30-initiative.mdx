## What is 30x30?

At COP15 in Montreal in December 2022, 196 nations agreed to the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. Its headline target: protect 30% of Earth's land, freshwater, and oceans by 2030. Currently, roughly 17% of land and 8% of the ocean are protected.

## Why 30%?

Scientists synthesised data from hundreds of species and ecosystems to determine that protecting 30% — particularly the most biodiversity-rich and carbon-dense ecosystems — can prevent up to 1 million species from going extinct and protect habitats of roughly two-thirds of terrestrial species.

## Progress so far

Costa Rica has over 25% of its territory under protection. The UK has designated new marine protected areas around its overseas territories. The United States launched the America the Beautiful domestic 30x30 initiative. Chile and Argentina have expanded marine protected areas in the Southern Ocean.

## Challenges and criticisms

The most significant concerns involve implementation and funding. The agreement committed to mobilise $200 billion annually for biodiversity by 2030 — progress toward this target has been slow. Research also shows that Indigenous-managed lands are among the most biodiverse on Earth, and critics warn that top-down approaches could repeat historical injustices.

## What effective protection looks like

Strictly protected core areas, buffer zones managed for compatible human use, wildlife corridors, Indigenous co-management with legally recognised land rights, and transparent monitoring with independent verification.
